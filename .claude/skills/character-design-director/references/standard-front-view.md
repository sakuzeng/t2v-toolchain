# Standard front-view anchor

Read this reference when creating the first stable full-body image for a character or normalizing a supplied character into a production anchor.

## Purpose

The standard front view is the primary identity and design anchor for later variants and reference sheets. It favors clarity, complete anatomy, and low perspective distortion over expressive posing.

## Default presentation

Use these defaults only when the user has not chosen another presentation:

- One character, full body, centered, whole head and feet visible
- Portrait canvas, commonly 2:3 for a full-body asset
- Head and neck upright, face directly forward, eyes level
- Shoulders and hips level; torso not twisted
- Arms relaxed with a small readable gap from the torso; hands neutral and visible
- Legs straight and uncrossed; stance natural for the design
- Footwear state exactly as specified; both feet supported naturally on one floor plane
- Eye- or chest-height long-lens/orthographic-like camera with minimal perspective distortion
- Plain light neutral background and soft even diffuse lighting
- No gesture, prop, wind, action, dramatic shadow, or environmental storytelling unless requested

These are asset-presentation rules, not permanent personality or anatomy traits.

## Proportion control

Specify only the proportions that matter: head-to-body impression, shoulder width, torso-to-leg balance, waist and hip relationship, build, and height impression. Do not assume that a stated real-world height will force the desired visual ratio. Review the generated silhouette rather than trusting numerical prompt claims.

Avoid low angles, wide lenses, crossed legs, raised heels, hip shifts, or fashion poses when accurate proportion reading matters.

## Standardization from an image

Preserve the supplied identity and design while separating transient presentation from character facts:

- A head tilt is usually pose, not identity.
- Perspective-shortened limbs are camera evidence, not true proportions.
- Windblown hair and fabric are transient unless named as a design feature.
- Occluded hands, feet, or back construction must not be invented with false certainty.

## Acceptance criteria

- Recognizable identity and correct medium
- Complete, readable silhouette
- Upright head and neutral anatomical stance
- Intended outfit and state, with separable garment construction
- No critical hand, foot, limb, crop, or perspective defects
- Suitable to serve as the sole primary anchor in a later prompt
